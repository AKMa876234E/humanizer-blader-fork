# Ironclad Insurance writing guidance (public-safe)

Use these rules only when the text is clearly for Ironclad Insurance Solutions, its prospects, clients, producers, advisors, or team. For unrelated or personal writing, use the normal Humanizer workflow without Ironclad branding.

## Voice and reader

- Write in plain English like a knowledgeable commercial insurance professional. Address a real reader, explain the next useful action, and avoid generic sales hype or dramatic promises.
- For insurance blogs, use clear headings, grounded examples, accurate qualifications, and natural SEO language. Do not repeat keywords at the expense of clarity.
- For client communication, make the coverage question, decision, requested document, or next action easy to identify. Keep calls to action specific and low pressure.
- Preserve a supplied voice sample, sender, recipient, and all verified contact details. Never invent signatures, testimonials, producer credentials, license numbers, or customer claims.
- Where the organization uses the role **Insurance Rep (IR)**, preserve that term instead of rewriting it as Account Executive (AE), unless quoting a source or using a third-party's exact title.

## Insurance accuracy comes before style

- Preserve every material coverage statement, exclusion, limit, sublimit, deductible, retention, attachment point, endorsement, class code, payroll amount, rating basis, effective or expiration date, carrier, policy reference, percentage, dollar figure, condition, qualifier, and citation.
- For General Liability, distinguish cross-liability or cross-action restrictions, action-over liability, designated operations, new construction restrictions, tract or residential development restrictions, project-size or square-footage limits, and public-building or government-work exposures. Their actual effect depends on the policy forms and endorsements; do not collapse them into an unsupported blanket exclusion.
- Do not change `may apply`, `subject to`, `as quoted`, or `confirm with carrier` into categorical coverage promises. No rewrite may imply that coverage was bound, approved, guaranteed, or verified when it was not.
- Keep a disclosed broker fee separate from premium or payroll-derived rates when it is identified as a separate flat policy cost. Do not quietly allocate it across rates or classes.
- Do not add or omit insurance claims, financial comparisons, legal conclusions, regulatory statements, or unsupported benefits. Request or verify missing facts when needed.
- Retain required disclaimers and **Professional Review Required** notices. Do not call a professional-review draft approved or distribution-ready.

## Brand presentation for files and artifacts

When an Ironclad document, slide, PDF, or other designed artifact is requested, use the official August 1, 2023 brand guidelines as controlling guidance. References and proposals cannot override them.

- Colors: navy `#001E33`, red `#F5404C`, cyan `#00AAC9`, light blue `#E5F2F4`, title `#27353F`, body `#5D6D7C`.
- Typography: P22 Mackinac Pro Medium for headlines, Montserrat SemiBold for subheads, Avenir Book for body text. If approved fonts are unavailable, substitute legibly and disclose the substitution.
- Use approved company logos only. Never redraw an official logo, alter its proportions, or claim approval of an unverified asset.
- Keep layouts spacious, readable, accessible, and consistent. Verify tables, source references, formulas, dates, links, and contrast before release.

These are editing guardrails, not an authorization to publish insurance advice or a substitute for carrier documents, legal review, or the official branding files. Do not put confidential client or internal operational information in this public repository.
